# Strategic Management & Business Analysis Portfolio (Wawel S.A.)

This repository contains comprehensive strategic management assignments and case studies analyzing the market position, macro-environment, internal potential, and ethical framework of **Wawel S.A.** – one of Poland's oldest confectionery manufacturers.

---

## 📋 Project Overview & Components

### 1. Porter's Five Forces Analysis
* **Industry Sector:** Confectionery market in Poland.
* **Evaluation:** Evaluated the overall sector attractiveness (average score of 2.86)[cite: 15], analyzing supplier bargaining power, buyer power, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, and competitive rivalry[cite: 14].
* **Key Finding:** High market saturation and strong rivalry require ongoing product innovation and clear differentiation (e.g., "clean label" strategy)[cite: 14, 15].

### 2. PEST Analysis & Scenario Planning
* **Macro-environment Factors:** Evaluated socio-cultural, technological, international, and politico-legal trends[cite: 19].
* **Scenario Development:** Formulated optimistic, pessimistic, and most probable scenarios for Wawel S.A. to assess future market risks and opportunities[cite: 19].

### 3. Internal Potential & BCG Matrix
* **Key Success Factors (KCS):** Weighted evaluation of internal strengths including market position, product quality, brand equity, distribution network, and production efficiency (total score: 3.60 / 5.0)[cite: 18].
* **BCG Matrix:** Portfolio analysis mapping product groups (such as chocolates and chocolate bars) based on market growth rate and relative market share[cite: 18].

### 4. SWOT Analysis
* **Strategic Position:** Maxi-Maxi strategy (strengths outweigh weaknesses; opportunities outweigh threats)[cite: 19].
* **Implications:** Focus on aggressive market penetration, expanding new product segments, and leveraging brand reputation and high-quality standards[cite: 19].

### 5. Business Ethics and Value Theory (Axiology)
* **Theoretical Framework:** Examination of axiology in management, exploring intrinsic, subjective, and objective theories of good, alongside major ethical frameworks (utilitarianism, deontological ethics, virtue ethics)[cite: 16, 17].
* **Case Study Comparison:** A comparative analysis of corporate responsibility contrasting **Patagonia** (ethics as a core strategy and driver of long-term trust) with **Volkswagen** (the Dieselgate scandal and the consequences of subordinating moral values to short-term profits)[cite: 16, 17].
